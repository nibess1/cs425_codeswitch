\paragraph{8-bit is safe.}
In every backend (LLM.int8, CTranslate2 int8, whisper.cpp Q8\_0) and model size, no 8-bit setting hurts embedded units more than matrix units: no 8-bit $R$ has an interval above 1, and the matrix change is mostly within noise.

\paragraph{4-bit hurts embedded units more in the smaller models.}
For fine-tuned tiny and base, 4-bit quantization raises embedded error 1.4 to 4.4 times as much as matrix error (Table~\ref{tab:R}; e.g.\ ft tiny NF4 on ASCEND: $R=3.47$ [1.85, 6.13]). This happens for both embedded English (ASCEND) and embedded Mandarin (NSC), and in both NF4 and whisper.cpp Q4\_0. On the corpora small is robust (but see \S\ref{sec:human}): its errors change by about one point and $R$ is about 1 or not reportable. Our off-the-shelf pilot had suggested the opposite ($R \approx 0.6$), but that does not carry over to fine-tuned models. In our per-block scan, the first encoder layer is always the most sensitive block.

\paragraph{Repairs work for NF4 but not for the deployed format.}
Keeping the two or three most sensitive blocks at 16-bit, or adding QLoRA adapters, recovers about half to nine-tenths of the NF4 embedded-error loss for tiny and full base (Table~\ref{tab:rec}); QLoRA does not help fine-tuned base, whose loss is small. When the QLoRA adapters are merged into the weights and the model is re-quantized to whisper.cpp Q4\_0, the recovery disappears: no slice has a Rec interval above 0, and for tiny on the Different Room test the merged model is slightly worse than plain Q4\_0. The adapters were trained against NF4's grid, not Q4\_0's block-wise one, and whisper.cpp cannot load mixed-precision files at all. In practice the answer for whisper.cpp is Q8\_0, which is as fast as Q4\_0 on our laptop (\S\ref{sec:deploy}) and changes embedded error by at most 1.2 points for any fine-tuned model.
